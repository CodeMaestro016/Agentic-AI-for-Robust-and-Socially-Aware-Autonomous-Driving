Research Methodology

Four independently developed research studies with distinct datasets, methods, and evaluation protocols.

Four studies, four experimental pipelines

The components share an academic theme, while each is developed and evaluated independently. They are research prototypes and do not constitute one tightly coupled production system.

Component 01

Adaptive-Intent Prediction in Occluded Urban Scenarios

Occlusion-aware pedestrian intention prediction with uncertainty-sensitive selective decision-making.

Video & tracking

PIE observations; runtime detection with a fine-tuned YOLO detector and BoT-SORT.

Occlusion evidence

Three-class occlusion estimator predicts no, partial, or full occlusion probabilities.

525-D representation

ResNet18 appearance + six spatial + four motion + three Bayesian reliability features.

30-frame sequence

Fixed temporal input preserves evolving appearance, motion and reliability.

Transformer intent

Outputs crossing probability from the temporal multimodal sequence.

Uncertainty

Monte Carlo dropout, entropy/variance measures, and temperature scaling.

Selective agent

MLP policy chooses OBSERVE_MORE, COMMIT_NOT_CROSSING, or COMMIT_CROSSING.

Explanation

Integrated Gradients groups evidence into intent, uncertainty, reliability and motion factors.

Evaluation protocol

Pedestrian-level separation was used to reduce identity leakage. The held-out test set contained 1,152 pedestrian sequences. Intent performance used accuracy, precision, recall, F1, ROC-AUC and AP; selective decisions used committed accuracy and coverage.

Component 02

Multi-Agent Negotiation for Right-of-Way in Complex Intersections

Autonomous-vehicle-only research prototype at an unsignalized four-way SUMO intersection, controlled through TraCI at a 0.04 s simulation step.

Ego-local observation

Each vehicle maintains its own Local Dynamic Map (LDM) and local maneuver-intention estimates.

Conflict relevance

Movement paths, 30 physical conflict zones, and temporal relevance identify relationships needing coordination.

Mandatory precedence

Traffic-rule precedence is resolved before reinforcement learning; structured precedence claims are exchanged.

Joint-local graph

Each ego owns its graph. An edge-aware message-passing representation uses 8-D nodes, 9-D edges, hidden dimension 64, and 3 layers.

Eligible negotiation

MAPPO handles only negotiation-eligible relationships using semantic actions and hard invalid-action masks.

Physical execution

Vehicle-owned decentralized execution converts resolved obligations to behavior while SUMO native safety behavior remains active.

Semantic negotiation actions

MAPPO operates on precedence claims
Role Actions
Proposer KEEP_CLAIM · RELINQUISH_CLAIM
Responder ACCEPT_RELINQUISHMENT · REJECT_RELINQUISHMENT

MAPPO does not directly select steering, brake, or throttle. Dense traffic can exceed four live vehicles, but learned negotiation is bounded to at most four physical queue-head participants, one per approach.

Component 03

Proactive Mental State Prediction Modeling using Theory of Mind

PIE pedestrian tracks are transformed into operational behavioral categories and rule-based TTC risk-aware decision support.

Feature extraction

MediaPipe-based pose landmarks support 36 features: 10 motion, 12 pose, 9 behavioral, and 5 contextual.

Temporal sequences

30 frames × 36 features, sliding-window stride 5 frames; track-disjoint 70:15:15 train / validation / test split.

Behavioral prediction

LSTM, GRU, and an equal-weight probability ensemble predict six operational categories.

Rule-based decision

Behavioral category and TTC jointly inform MAINTAIN, SLOW_DOWN, or STOP.

Six operational behavioral states

Waiting Hesitant Committed Distracted Aggressive Jaywalk

These categories are operational behavioral proxies; they are not verified psychological mental states.

TTC risk thresholds — behavioral state also informs the final rule mechanism
TTC Risk Action
TTC > 2.5 s Low MAINTAIN
1.5 s < TTC ≤ 2.5 s Warning SLOW_DOWN
TTC ≤ 1.5 s Critical STOP

Multiple pedestrians

Each pedestrian is assessed and the overall response takes the most restrictive required action: STOP > SLOW_DOWN > MAINTAIN. CARLA evaluation includes six single-pedestrian scenarios and one multi-pedestrian scenario, with 16 spawned and at most five simultaneously visible pedestrians.

The mechanism is rule-based decision support. CARLA establishes simulation-level technical feasibility; the multi-pedestrian scenario supplies no quantitative classification or collision-avoidance metric.
Component 04

Agentic Knowledge Sharing for Federated Collective Learning in Autonomous Vehicles

Each vehicle retains its own MobileNetV2 traffic-sign classifier. Selective exchange uses verified embeddings and metadata.

Shared detection

YOLOv8n identifies the traffic-sign region; the vehicle-specific MobileNetV2 classifier estimates confidence.

Confidence categories

KNOWN ≥ 0.70; RARE ≥ 0.30 and < 0.70; NEW < 0.30. Rare/new observations check local temporary memory first.

Memory and DQN

Memory cosine threshold is 0.70. Without a match, a pretrained DQN selects SHARE or IGNORE.

Importance score

Importance = 0.83 × visual prominence + 0.17 × novelty.

Compact package

An embedding and metadata are shared. Raw camera images and classifier weights are excluded.

Server verification

Cosine similarity ≥ 0.70 against a 43-class reference store is required for acceptance into the broadcast queue.

Manual broadcast

Broadcast is manually triggered. Vehicles save accepted packages in separate temporary memories; receipt does not update classifiers immediately.

Later local retraining

The MobileNetV2 base is frozen and the existing 43-class classification head is updated locally.

Evaluation protocol

The same 12-class single-sign crop set, prepared from 300 held-out GTSDB scenes, evaluates three specialist vehicles. CARLA Town01 demonstrates integration; it is not the source of accuracy figures.

Observed accuracy changes belong to the combined sharing + memory + local retraining workflow. No controlled ablation isolates individual stages. Vehicle C completed Test 2 but not Test 3.
