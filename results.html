Main finding: GRU achieved the highest macro-F1 even though the ensemble achieved the highest accuracy and weighted F1. The ensemble is not best on every metric.

CARLA feasibility demonstration

Six single-pedestrian scenarios and one multi-pedestrian scenario were used. The multi-pedestrian scenario spawned 16 pedestrians, with at most five simultaneously visible. Configured states included Committed, Waiting, Hesitant, Distracted, and Jaywalk.

The rule-based TTC risk-aware decision mechanism produced MAINTAIN, SLOW_DOWN, and STOP. Across pedestrians, the most restrictive required action governs: STOP > SLOW_DOWN > MAINTAIN.

Limitation: The multi-pedestrian scenario did not establish quantitative classification accuracy or collision-avoidance performance. These results demonstrate simulation-based technical feasibility only; real-world safety and generalization remain unverified.

Agentic Knowledge Sharing for Federated Collective Learning in Autonomous Vehicles

Main finding: Accuracy changes reflect the combined workflow; no ablation isolated individual causal contributions. Improvement belongs to sharing, temporary memory, and later local retraining together; it cannot be attributed to DQN, server verification, or sharing alone.

Scope and limitations

Only 12 selected classes were scored, although the reference store and classification heads cover 43 classes. CARLA Town01 provides an integration demonstration, not the accuracy figures. Broadcast is manually triggered; receiving a package does not immediately update a classifier. Vehicle C did not complete Test 3.
