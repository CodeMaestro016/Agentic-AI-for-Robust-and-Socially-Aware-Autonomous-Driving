Research domain

Research Domain / Literature Survey

The project sits at the intersection of autonomous driving, pedestrian behaviour understanding, multi-agent decision-making, explainable AI, reinforcement learning, and privacy-aware collaborative learning.

Urban autonomous driving requires more than object detection. Vehicles must interpret intent under partial evidence, reason about rules and conflicts, respond to nuanced human behaviour, and coordinate or learn with other intelligent agents.

The four studies explore these challenges at different levels: pedestrian perception, intersection negotiation, social reasoning, and fleet-level knowledge exchange. Together, they form a broader investigation into adaptive and explainable agentic behaviour for autonomous vehicles.

Literature survey summary

Literature Survey

Pedestrian understanding

Prior work has established temporal and multimodal approaches for crossing-intention prediction, including appearance, motion, pose, contextual cues, attention, graph models, and uncertainty estimation. The remaining challenge is dependable decision-making when visibility is degraded and evidence changes over time.

Intersection coordination

Multi-agent reinforcement learning, graph-based interaction models, trajectory coordination, and rule-aware approaches provide foundations for unsignalized-intersection management. Remaining difficulties include partial observation, uncertain intentions, simultaneous conflicts, hard right-of-way constraints, and liveness.

Socially compliant AV behaviour

Pedestrian-intention research often frames the problem as crossing versus not crossing. Theory-of-Mind-inspired work motivates richer behavioural states so that an AV can distinguish waiting, hesitation, distraction, abnormal behaviour, and jaywalking and respond more proactively.

Collaborative vehicle learning

Federated learning and knowledge-distillation approaches reduce the need to centralise raw data, but commonly exchange model updates or train toward a shared model. A separate question is whether an individual observation is valuable enough to share at all.

Research gap

Research Gap

Overall gap. Existing autonomous-driving research offers strong solutions for individual perception, prediction, coordination, and collaborative learning tasks, but complex urban operation still requires systems that can adapt to incomplete evidence, negotiate under safety and rule constraints, reason about human behavioural states, and exchange useful knowledge selectively rather than treating every observation or model update equally.
Component 01

Occlusion-aware intent prediction

Conventional intent classifiers are typically expected to return a committed prediction even when partial occlusion removes important appearance or motion evidence. The study addresses the need to combine visibility reliability, calibrated uncertainty, and the option to defer a decision.

Component 02

Rule-constrained multi-agent negotiation

Learning-based coordination at unsignalized intersections must cope with partial observations, intention uncertainty, multiple physical conflicts, and mandatory traffic-rule precedence. The research separates non-negotiable rule and safety constraints from learned negotiation.

Component 03

Beyond binary pedestrian intent

Binary crossing / not-crossing formulations can overlook meaningful intermediate or risky behavioural states. The study explores six operational behavioral categories and an interpretable link from inferred state and time-to-collision to proactive AV action.

Component 04

Selective knowledge sharing

Conventional federated averaging shares model updates to support one common model. This study instead asks whether one uncertain traffic-sign observation should be shared, verifies compact embeddings before broadcast, and lets each vehicle retain its own specialist classifier.

Research problem & proposed solution

Research Problem

01 · Occluded pedestrian intention

Problem: Partial occlusion degrades the visual evidence needed to infer whether a pedestrian will cross, while classifier confidence may remain misleading.

Solution: Fuse appearance, spatial, motion, and Bayesian observation-reliability features over 30 frames, predict intent with a Transformer, estimate uncertainty, and use a learned selective policy to commit or observe more.

02 · Right-of-way at complex intersections

Problem: Multiple AVs at uncontrolled intersections must infer intentions, identify actual conflict zones, obey mandatory precedence, and coordinate without relying on a complete global state.

Solution: Build ego-local maps, estimate intentions with two GRU models, construct rule- and conflict-aware precedence graphs, and negotiate eligible relationships using graph-based MAPPO.

03 · Proactive social compliance

Problem: A reactive planner that only considers trajectory or binary crossing status cannot distinguish nuanced pedestrian states requiring different anticipatory actions.

Solution: Extract 36 pose, motion, behavioural, and contextual features, classify six operational behavioral categories with LSTM/GRU models and an ensemble, then apply a rule-based TTC risk-aware decision mechanism.

04 · Fleet knowledge sharing

Problem: A useful traffic sign learned by one vehicle may remain unavailable to others, while continuous model-update exchange can be costly and removes local specialisation.

Solution: Detect and classify locally, let a DQN decide SHARE / IGNORE for rare or new signs, verify shared embeddings centrally, broadcast accepted knowledge, and retrain each local classifier later.

Research objectives

Research Objectives

Separate objectives for four independently developed research studies.

Component 01

  • Predict crossing intention under partial pedestrian visibility.
  • Represent observation reliability probabilistically rather than as a hard visibility label.
  • Estimate and calibrate predictive uncertainty.
  • Learn when to commit to CROSSING / NOT CROSSING or request further evidence.
  • Provide evidence-based explanations and demonstrate raw-video operation.

Component 02

  • Construct decentralized ego-local representations of complex intersections.
  • Predict surrounding-vehicle manoeuvres before conflict resolution.
  • Combine physical conflict zones and traffic-rule precedence.
  • Learn negotiable precedence relationships with graph-based MAPPO.
  • Evaluate vehicle-owned SUMO execution for safety and liveness separately.

Component 03

  • Develop a reproducible 36-feature pedestrian-state representation.
  • Study six operational behavioral proxies, not verified psychological mental states.
  • Benchmark LSTM and GRU temporal classifiers using identical data splits.
  • Combine predictions with TTC in a rule-based TTC risk-aware decision mechanism.
  • Support explanations and multi-pedestrian threat prioritisation.

Component 04

  • Support selective sign-level knowledge sharing among vehicles.
  • Keep raw images and local classifier weights on the vehicle.
  • Verify submitted embeddings before broadcast.
  • Reuse accepted knowledge through temporary memory and later local retraining.
  • Demonstrate the complete path in CARLA and evaluate classifier changes on held-out traffic-sign crops.

YOLOv8n, separate MobileNetV2 classifiers, temporary memory, DQN sharing, server verification, manual broadcast, and later local retraining.

Scope and limitations

These studies demonstrate bounded research and simulation-level feasibility. Component 1 retains its verified paper evidence; its supplied thesis is available in Documents. Component 2 exposes dense-traffic deadlock despite zero observed collisions. Component 3 uses behavioral proxies and has no verified real-world safety or generalization claim. Component 4 scores 12 selected classes, and no ablation isolates causal contributions within its combined workflow.
